"Tamil Nadu is losing its waterbodies at an alarming rate. Encroachments (illegal constructions, land filling, sand mining and waste dumping) go undetected for months because: (1) traditional systems rely on public complaints, which are often unreported or unnoticed; (2) manual inspection is slow, costly and geographically limited; (3) by the time authorities act, the damage is irreversible. Consequences: flooding during heavy rains, reduced groundwater recharge, and permanent loss of water sources for future generations." A side-by-side comparison of the three structural points of failure in conventional revenue administration vs. AquaCop's automated sentinel architecture.

01
Traditional Failure Mode

Relies on Citizen Complaints

Problems remain unnoticed for months. Remote wetland margins, canal culverts, and reservoir perimeters have zero resident footfall. Public grievances are delayed by 60 to 90 days.

✓ AquaCop's Answer

Autonomous scanning every few days. Sentinel-2 multi-spectral sensors scan all monitored waterbodies systematically. Public complaints become corroborating field evidence, not the initial trigger.

02
Traditional Failure Mode

Manual Field Inspection is Slow

Taluk and revenue surveyors must physically traverse tens of thousands of hectares. Field visits are expensive, logistically constrained, and easily delayed by procedural backlog.

✓ AquaCop's Answer

One centralized system covers all monitored bodies. Officers are dispatched solely to verified anomaly alerts, prioritized by Disaster Risk Score, eliminating blind patrol rounds.

03
Traditional Failure Mode

Action Occurs After Permanent Damage

By the time revenue notices are processed, concrete footings are cast, illegal buildings are inhabited, and court injunctions stall recovery. Damaged flood corridors cannot be easily cleared.

✓ AquaCop's Answer

Time-to-detection measured in hours to days. AquaCop logs the date the first change appeared in satellite imagery vs date flagged vs date acted, preventing irreversible construction.

Multi-Spectral Change Classification

Four Threats We Watch நாம் கண்காணிக்கும் 4 முக்கிய அச்சுறுத்தல்கள்

Implemented as separate change classes in the detection service with distinct map signatures and honest confidence ratings. Detections for sand mining and waste dumping are never presented as certain without ground checks.

High Confidence

Illegal Construction

What it looks like from space:

New high-reflectance built-up geometry, masonry plinths, and roof structures appearing inside the waterbody or its indicative buffer boundary.

Detection method:

NDBI (SWIR - NIR)/(SWIR + NIR) increase + OSM building layers.

Direct eviction notice eligible after field check.
High Confidence

Land Filling & Infill

What it looks like from space:

Permanent conversion of open water or marsh spread to compacted bare soil, red earth mounds, and boundary embankments.

Detection method:

MNDWI negative transition (>0.30 down to <-0.10) verified across 2+ orbit passes.

Flags earth-moving machinery within days.
Medium — Needs Field Check

Sand Mining & Dredging

What it looks like from space:

New high-albedo bare-sand patches, excavated dredged pits, altered channel beds, and vehicle tire haulage tracks in river or reservoir beds.

Detection method:

Sentinel-2 Bare Soil Index (BSI) + Sentinel-1 SAR surface roughness changes.

Never presented as certain without ground check.
Low to Med — Candidate, Verify

Waste & Effluent Dumping

What it looks like from space:

Unusual dark or heterogeneous spectral discolouration along water edges, perimeter debris plumes, or localized turbidity spikes.

Detection method:

Spectral edge gradient anomaly (Band 04 Red / Band 08 NIR ratio) along boundary.

Best confirmed by citizen geotagged photos.

A seamless statutory bridge between space-borne Earth observation and zonal revenue enforcement.

Watch

Near real-time (~5 day revisit)

Copernicus Sentinel-2 MSI constellation passes every ~5 days, acquiring 10m resolution multi-spectral imagery across Green (B03), Red (B04), NIR (B08), and SWIR (B11).

Detect

Multi-spectral change pipeline

Automated algorithms compute MNDWI and NDBI against 6-month seasonal baselines and 38-year JRC historical occurrence, categorizing anomalies into the 4 threat classes.

Alert

DEM & OSM building risk scoring

Source: Copernicus DEM + OSM

Act and Track

Section 133 CrPC Eviction Pack

Officers receive geo-tagged dossier with cadastral plot numbers. Official eviction notices are generated with QR code evidence packs. Detection delay and resolution status are tracked.

Source: Government of Tamil Nadu WRD
Scientific Integrity & Transparency

About the Data

Every number and layer on AquaCop originates from verified public space agencies, meteorological institutes, and open-source geographic foundations.

Copernicus Sentinel-2 MSI Level-2A
Provider: European Space Agency (ESA)
Resolution: 10m (B02, B03, B04, B08) & 20m (B11)
Revisit: ~5 days orbital pass (cloud-free)

Primary surface water loss, vegetation shrinkage, and construction detection.

EC JRC Global Surface Water (1984–2021)
Provider: European Commission Joint Research Centre
Resolution: 30m Landsat 38-year permanent record
Revisit: Permanent historical occurrence benchmark

Differentiates permanent historical encroachment from seasonal variations.

Copernicus 30m Global DEM
Provider: Open-Meteo Elevation API / Copernicus
Resolution: 30m horizontal grid, 1m vertical
Revisit: Static digital elevation model

Terrain slopes, depression bowls, and downstream flood hazard runoffs.

OpenStreetMap (OSM) Overpass API
Provider: OpenStreetMap Community (ODbL License)
Resolution: Vector polygon cadastral geometries
Revisit: Continuous community/government updates

Indicative buffer zones (50m) and downstream residential structure counts.

Open-Meteo Global Forecasting API
Provider: ECMWF & Global Meteorological Models
Resolution: 0.1° atmospheric grid
Revisit: Updated every 6 hours

Correlates water spread loss with rainfall to rule out drought false-positives.

Esri World Imagery Basemap
Provider: Maxar / Earthstar Geographics / Esri
Resolution: Sub-meter aerial photography tiles
Revisit: Periodic high-resolution photographic mosaic

Photographic reference background for Leaflet GIS overlays.

Statutory Limitations & Verification Requirements:
  • Indicative Boundaries: All waterbody boundaries and buffer lines shown on satellite overlays are indicative reference boundaries derived from OpenStreetMap and satellite water occurrence until digitized state revenue cadastral maps (FMB sketches) are formally integrated.
  • Near Real-Time Revisit: Satellites pass every ~5 days. Heavy monsoon cloud cover (optical occlusion > 20%) can delay clear optical acquisition until the subsequent orbital pass.
  • Mandatory Field Verification: Sand mining and waste dumping detections are spectral candidates (medium/low-medium confidence) and require mandatory ground truth verification by Zonal Revenue Officers before punitive statutory eviction under Section 133 CrPC is finalized.
